# Humans as Attack Vectors

## Overview / Objective
Focus on the weakest link in cybersecurity, people, and on how SOC teams detect and respond to social-engineering attacks.

## Platform & date
| Platform | Module | Completed |
|---|---|---|
| TryHackMe | Humans as Attack Vectors | 30 August 2026 |

## What I did / key steps
- **Social engineering:** how attackers bypass technical controls by manipulating human psychology through trust and urgency
- **Common vectors:** phishing emails, malware downloads through fake software captchas, deepfakes and executive impersonation
- **Mitigation vs detection:** security awareness training and anti-phishing tools, alongside EDR and SOC alert monitoring
- **Hands-on lab:** acting as a Junior SOC Analyst, I handled realistic workplace incidents:
  - blocked malicious email attachments
  - quarantined suspicious software
  - disabled compromised accounts

## Findings / outcome
Completed the lab scenarios using the response actions listed above.

## MITRE ATT&CK mapping
| Technique | Relevance |
|---|---|
| T1566 Phishing | Phishing emails and malicious attachments |

## Lessons learned
Technical controls are not enough on their own. Defence against social engineering combines awareness training, anti-phishing tools, EDR and SOC monitoring.

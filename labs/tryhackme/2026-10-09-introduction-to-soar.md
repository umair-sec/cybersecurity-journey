# Introduction to SOAR

## Overview / Objective
Learn how Security Orchestration, Automation and Response (SOAR) helps with common SOC problems: alert fatigue, disconnected security tools, repetitive manual processes and growing analyst workload.

## Platform & date
| Platform | Room | Path | Completed |
|---|---|---|---|
| TryHackMe | SOAR | SOC Level 1 | 9 October 2026 |

## What I did / key steps
- Reviewed traditional SOC processes and their challenges
- Studied Security Orchestration, Automation and Response
- Worked with SOAR playbooks and decision-based workflows
- Walked through **phishing** and **CVE patching** playbooks
- Looked at threat intelligence integration
- Decided which workflow steps should be manual and which automated
- Practised case management, reputation checks and incident data extraction
- Covered Course of Action (COA) and analyst validation

## Key concepts
- Playbooks and decision-based workflows
- Threat intelligence and reputation-check enrichment
- Case management
- Course of Action (COA)
- Human validation and approval steps

## Findings / outcome
The practical exercise showed that **not everything that can be automated should be automated**. Validation, approvals, sensitive analysis and critical response decisions still need human judgement.

## Lessons learned
SOAR reduces repetitive work. It doesn't replace the SOC analyst.
